## What is being decided, and the standard it must meet

We are looking for a domain in which to reproduce the *structure* of the FDM-1 result:
an abundant unlabelled corpus, a mechanism that manufactures labels for it at near-zero
marginal cost, a known-good recipe, and a quality curve that rises with data. Five hard
gates, all of which must pass:

| Gate | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **G1** | ≥ ~10⁶ units exist and are legally obtainable, with a named source and a count. |
| **G2** | A label manufacturer at near-zero marginal cost: (a) inverse model on a small seed, (b) environment supplies truth free, or (c) the artifact was generated from a structured source that still exists. |
| **G3** | More data/compute plausibly makes it better, **and** a bigger general model with plain prompting does not simply do it. |
| **G4** | White space verified, not assumed: < ~3 serious efforts and no open dataset at target scale, after ≥6 query formulations and ≥2 domain-specific venues. |
| **G5** | If it works at scale, a named buyer type pays. |

Two further preconditions were added before any candidate was scored, so that they could
not be invented afterwards to justify a choice:

| Gate | Requirement | Why it was added |
|---|---|---|
| **G6** | The four-rung curve must be trainable on the actual execution box: 4 CPU cores, 15 GB RAM, **no GPU**, ~30 GB disk. | This is the machine the work runs on. A domain that needs a GPU cluster is not a domain this project can execute. |
| **G2a** | The label manufacturer must know something the student's input cannot contain. | Added *after* round 1 on the evidence below. See “The finding that changed the gates”. |

## The finding that changed the gates

VPT worked for a reason that is easy to state and easy to miss. The inverse dynamics model
saw **future frames**. The student, at inference time, sees only the past. So the IDM held
information the student's input could not contain, and distilling it pulled the student
toward a target above its own reach.

Round 1 produced a candidate in which that relationship is exactly inverted, and it took an
adversarial reviewer measuring the real data to see it. The serial-crystallography proposal
was to run CrystFEL across the public archive and treat every pattern it successfully indexes
as a free labelled example. But **CrystFEL sees precisely the same peak list the student
sees.** There is no extra information anywhere in the loop. Training on those labels is
distillation, and distillation ceilings at the teacher. Worse, the commercial claim was to
index the 33–50% of patterns classical indexers *discard* — which is exactly the population
for which no label can be manufactured. The manufacturer is definitionally blind to the
population the thesis is about.

That failure is invisible to G1–G5 as originally written. A mechanism can be abundant, free,
legal, uncrowded and commercially attractive, and still be worthless because it cannot teach
anything its student could not already have learned. So it becomes a gate, and it is applied
from here on:

> **G2a — INFORMATION ASYMMETRY.** The label manufacturer must have access to information the
> student's input does not contain. If the manufacturer sees exactly what the student sees,
> the study is distillation, its ceiling is the teacher, and the curve flattens there.

### Three more failure modes round 1 surfaced

Each of these killed a candidate, each was found by measurement rather than argument, and
each is cheap enough to test that it should be tested *first* from now on.

**1. Zero channel capacity — the label is not a function of the input.** The JVM reviewer
downloaded four JDKs, compiled a real 345-class library under each, and byte-compared the
output. `javac 17.0.8` and `javac 17.0.13` at `--release 8` produced 345 of 345 byte-identical
class files. Not "hard to distinguish" — *identical*. The proposed headline label carries zero
bits, and no quantity of data raises a curve over a channel of zero capacity.
→ *Before anything else, verify the label actually varies with the input.*

**2. Heuristic-shaped labels.** The CopybookLM reviewer wrote a ~50-line rule with no learning
in it and reached exact-boundary F1 0.768 from 20 records; 20,000 records bought 1.4 points.
Most of the label mass was a regex over EBCDIC nibble ranges. A manufactured label that a
regex mostly solves leaves a curve drawn over a residual.
→ *Write the dumbest possible non-learned baseline first, and measure it before building anything.*

**3. Corpus terms that forbid this specific use.** Maven Central's `robots.txt` reads
`Content-Signal: search=yes,ai-train=no,use=reference` followed by `Disallow: /`. G1 is not
"can the bytes be fetched". It is "may we use them this way, and may we redistribute what we
build" — because Phase 3 requires shipping an open artifact.
→ *Read the actual terms of the actual corpus before scoring G1, not after.*

## Where round 1 leaves us

**No candidate cleared all the gates.** Of four finalists reviewed adversarially: zero SELECT,
two BACKUP, two REJECT. The two rejections were decisive and were established by measurement.
The two BACKUPs each survive only in a narrowed regime:

- **Compressed-stream encoder provenance** survives only for *carved fragments with no
  plaintext and no stream start*, because every incumbent tool — `preflate`, `precomp`,
  `grittibanzli`, `list-compresslevel.py` — needs one or the other, and brute-force
  recompression needs the plaintext. That narrowing is real and the reviewer measured signal
  in it (trivial baselines at 0.235 against 0.111 chance; unscaled logistic regression at
  chance across every feature set). But the reviewer also found that the white-space claim as
  originally written is falsified by `microsoft/preflate-rs`, which fingerprints encoder
  implementation and parameters in production. G4 is weaker than the round-1 score suggests.
- **Serial-crystallography indexing** fails the new G2a outright, as set out above.

### This is not "no viable domain found" — yet

That verdict is a permitted and legitimate terminal outcome, and it will be issued if the
evidence supports it. It does not yet, in both directions:

1. **Two screened survivors were never adversarially tested.** *Headerless load-layout
   recovery* and *ProcedureNet* were cut by a mechanical top-4 slice, not by a judgement.
   Neither has been attacked.
2. **Two of the four adversarial reviews ran with their web-search quota exhausted** and fell
   back to direct arXiv/Europe PMC fetches. Their on-box measurements are first-hand and
   strong; their external-evidence coverage is weaker than it looks, and is recorded as such.
3. **The candidate pool was generated before G2a existed.** A pool generated *with* the
   asymmetry requirement, and with these three failure modes named as explicit anti-patterns,
   is a materially different search.

Declaring "no viable domain" on evidence this incomplete would be exactly the kind of
confident-but-unearned claim this project exists to avoid. Round 2 closes these three gaps
specifically. If round 2 also returns no SELECT, that is the answer and it will be written
down as the answer.

## Round 1: 7 survivors carried to white-space verification

### 1. Inverse Linking: compile-unit partitioning of stripped binaries

Given a stripped executable's raw code bytes, recover the object-file (compile-unit) partition it was linked from, plus a coarse source-identity class per unit.

- **Input modality** — Raw machine-code byte windows (512-2048B) plus address-ordered relocation/xref/call-graph features from a stripped ELF/PE. Not disassembly prose, not text.
- **Label** — Per-function compile-unit id (a partition over the binary's functions), CU count, CU boundary offsets, coarse source-file/namespace class per unit.
- **Corpus (G1)** — Assemblage corpus (arXiv 2405.03991): 428,884 Linux ELF + 890,000 Windows PE binaries built from 30,856 GitHub repos with recorded build configs. Plus distro debug-symbol archives, free and bulk-downloadable: deb.debian.org/debian-debug, debuginfod.debian.net, Ubuntu ddebs, Fedora debuginfod (Debian bookworm alone: 64,419 binary packages / 34,790 source packages). Unit = function-with-CU-label; a mid-size binary has 10^3-10^4 functions, so >=10^7 labelled units is conservative.
- **Manufacturer (G2, shape `c-generated-from-structured-source`)** — DWARF is the structured source that still exists. .debug_info records, for free, the compile unit each function came from (DW_TAG_compile_unit ranges, DW_AT_decl_file) and which regions are inlined callees. Manufacture: download a debuginfo binary, walk DWARF with pyelftools to get the exact CU partition, then `objcopy --strip-all` and keep the stripped bytes as input. Milliseconds per unit, no human, no seed model. Critically there is NO circularity: the labels come from the symbol table, not from the heuristic tool that is the baseline.
- **Scale argument (G3)** — The label is a partition over thousands of functions expressed over byte offsets in a multi-megabyte binary. There is no prompt that expresses it and no context window that holds it. Scale monotonicity is physical: each additional distro release / architecture / compiler generation adds a new linker layout regime (section ordering, --gc-sections, LTO partitioning, identical-code-folding, PGO reordering) and the model's job is exactly to learn the joint distribution over those regimes. The regimes that break the trivial baseline (LTO, ICF, BOLT/PGO reordering) are the ones becoming MORE common over time, so the thesis gets stronger as toolchains improve rather than weaker as models improve.
- **Buyer (G5)** — Binary SBOM / software-composition-analysis vendors (FOSSA, Finite State, ONEKEY, NetRise) who must attribute a CVE to the specific vendored object file inside a stripped firmware blob rather than to the whole image — a requirement being hardened by the EU Cyber Resilience Act's firmware SBOM obligations.
- **Why it survived the screen** — Best-balanced candidate: verified 10^7-scale legally-obtainable corpus, a zero-cost exact label manufacturer with no circularity, a label shape that is literally inexpressible as a prompt, thin verified white space (REcover's own abstract says the problem 'has not been sufficiently studied'), a named buyer under regulatory pressure, and an ideal CPU profile. The one real risk to design around is the address-contiguity baseline — the study must report boundary F1 against 'every contiguous run is one unit' and must weight the eval toward LTO/ICF/reordered binaries. Note adjacency to the headerless-firmware candidate below: same corpus, different label; do not run both as one study.

### 2. Compressed-stream encoder provenance: which implementation and settings produced this bitstream

From the entropy-coded bytes of a DEFLATE/zstd/brotli/LZMA stream, identify the encoder implementation, version bucket and parameter tuple — precisely enough to recompress byte-identically.

- **Input modality** — Raw compressed bitstream bytes (Huffman/ANS-coded, near-maximum entropy). Categorically outside any text pretraining distribution; a text tokenizer destroys the signal by construction.
- **Label** — Encoder family + implementation (zlib / zlib-ng / libdeflate / zopfli / 7-Zip / igzip / Java Deflater / Go flate / Apple / Cloudflare fork), version bucket, level, strategy, window/chunk parameters; stretch head = the exact argv that reproduces the stream bit-for-bit.
- **Corpus (G1)** — The streams already exist in the billions: Software Heritage holds 27 billion unique source files from 421 million projects; Govdocs1 is ~1 million freely redistributable documents (digitalcorpora.org); every .deb/.whl/.jar/.apk/PNG/ZIP in the Debian archive (64,419 packages) contains DEFLATE streams. Plaintext feedstock for labelled generation is unbounded from the same sources; 10^6 labelled streams is minutes of CPU.
- **Manufacturer (G2, shape `b-environment-free-truth`)** — The compressor is the oracle. Take any byte string, run it through ~10 encoder implementations x ~10 levels x ~4 strategies and you have ~400 perfectly labelled streams per input at microsecond cost. The label is genuinely NOT in the artifact: raw DEFLATE has no header at all, and zlib's FLEVEL field is 2 advisory bits that are routinely wrong or absent — so this is a real inverse problem, not metadata reading. Ground truth is exact and free forever, and every new encoder release extends the label set at zero cost.
- **Scale argument (G3)** — The input is a maximum-entropy bitstream whose signal lives in block-split boundaries, Huffman table shapes, match-length/distance histograms and lazy-matching tie-break artefacts. Plain prompting is not merely weak, it is inexpressible — there is no legible text. Discriminating two encoders that differ only in match-finder tie-breaking is a pure statistical problem that gets strictly easier with more streams and more content diversity, and a frontier model getting bigger does not move it at all because the modality is wrong. The framing must be forensic (carved/truncated fragments, plaintext unavailable, encoders outside the candidate set) so the brute-force recompression oracle is not the comparison.
- **Buyer (G5)** — Digital-forensics tooling vendors (Magnet Forensics, Cellebrite) and national archives doing fragment attribution, plus reproducible-builds and CDN-dedup engineering teams (Debian reproducible builds, Chainguard, Cloudflare) who must re-emit byte-identical ZIP/gzip/jar artefacts and today guess the original compressor settings by hand.
- **Why it survived the screen** — Strongest G3 modality argument in the entire pool — a frontier model literally cannot represent the input — combined with the cheapest label manufacturer and the best G6 profile (generate, featurise, discard; disk pressure ~zero). Verified white space: only algorithm-FAMILY detection exists (arXiv 2111.09034, FiFTy-style fragment typing), nothing at implementation/parameter granularity. The known kill (recompress-and-compare when you hold the plaintext) is real but bounded, and the study must headline the fragment/no-plaintext setting where that oracle does not exist.

### 3. JVM bytecode build-provenance: which toolchain actually produced this class file

From .class bytecode alone, predict which compiler (javac vendor and point version, ecj, kotlinc), which build tool and which flags produced it — and flag artifacts whose bytecode contradicts their declared provenance.

- **Input modality** — JVM bytecode structure: constant-pool ordering, attribute layout, synthetic/bridge generation, exception-table shape, StackMapTable frame encoding, local-variable slot allocation. Structured binary, only semi-legible, reduced to a compact token sequence.
- **Label** — Compiler implementation + point version, build tool + plugin version, -source/-target, -g debug level, -parameters, shading/relocation; primary head = binary 'declared provenance is inconsistent with the bytecode'.
- **Corpus (G1)** — Maven Central, publicly and bulk-downloadable. A 2025 Zenodo extraction covers >=16.5 million POMs; mvnrepository reports ~69 million indexed artifacts (single source, unverified); a 2025 dataset covers 122,201 artifacts released in one calendar year. Unit = a .class file and a typical jar holds 10^2-10^3 classes, so even the conservative single-year slice gives >10^7 class-level units.
- **Manufacturer (G2, shape `c-generated-from-structured-source`)** — The jar carries its own answer key and you delete it. META-INF/MANIFEST.MF records Created-By, Build-Jdk, Build-Jdk-Spec, Build-Tool; maven-archiver writes pom.properties. Strip those files, keep the bytecode, and you have a perfectly labelled pair at zero marginal cost, 10^7 times over. Secondary manufacturer for rare versions: recompile source-available projects under a matrix of JDKs in Docker.
- **Scale argument (G3)** — The target is a point version (javac 11.0.2 vs 11.0.9) and a flag set, distinctions invisible to reasoning and visible only in codegen micro-quirks — constant-pool entry ordering, when a synthetic accessor is emitted, StackMapTable frame-compression choices. That is an empirical fingerprint whose resolution improves monotonically with the number of (artifact, toolchain) pairs; more versions in the label set means finer discrimination, not confusion. A bigger general model has no path to these quirks because they are not described anywhere in text — the field's own consensus is that the task is impossible.
- **Buyer (G5)** — Software supply-chain and build-attestation vendors (Chainguard, Sonatype, JFrog, Google Assured OSS) verifying that a published artifact matches its claimed build environment — the technical core of SLSA provenance for the 10^7 pre-existing Maven artifacts that have no attestation at all.
- **Why it survived the screen** — The best-verified white space in the pool: searches return only how-to articles and version-lookup tables, with the community's explicit answer being 'you cannot determine the exact javac version from bytecode' — zero ML attempts, no dataset. Corpus is enormous, legally obtainable and self-labelling; G6 is excellent. Mandatory reframing to survive G3: the class-file version bytes leak the -target JDK for free, so the headline must be tamper/inconsistency detection (bytecode vs declared manifest), not 'guess the JDK', and the eval must include artifacts whose -source/-target decouples class-file version from actual compiler.

### 4. Headerless load-layout recovery: base address, ISA and section segmentation for raw firmware blobs

Given a firmware blob with no ELF/PE header, recover its load base address, architecture, endianness, entry point and code/data/rodata segmentation.

- **Input modality** — Raw byte stream plus derived pointer-value histograms and instruction-alignment statistics. No headers, no metadata, no text.
- **Label** — Base address (the hard one), ISA + endianness + word size, entry point, and per-offset code/data/rodata/string-table/padding segmentation.
- **Corpus (G1)** — Manufactured by deletion from binaries that already exist: Assemblage (428,884 Linux ELF + 890,000 Windows PE, arXiv 2405.03991) is already >10^6 units before any cross-compilation; Debian bookworm's 64,419 packages across ~8 architectures; plus cross-compilation of Software Heritage sources to 20+ long-tail ISAs (RISC-V, Xtensa, ARC, MIPS variants). Real vendor firmware images reserved as held-out eval.
- **Manufacturer (G2, shape `c-generated-from-structured-source`)** — The header IS the label, so you manufacture the task by deleting it. Read an ELF/PE's true load address, entry point, architecture and section table with pyelftools, then strip all headers and optionally relocate the image to a random base — you now have a headerless blob whose every answer you know exactly. Cost: one header read plus one memcpy per unit. The cleanest manufacturer in the pool: the labels already sit inside 10^6+ public artefacts and no tool, model or human is in the loop.
- **Scale argument (G3)** — Base-address recovery is a global inverse over the whole blob: the correct base is the one under which the largest number of embedded 32/64-bit words become valid pointers into strings, code and tables. No prompt formulation exists, no context window holds 8MB, and the input has no text. Scaling is monotone and directly testable: coverage of more architectures, ABIs and compiler pointer-emission idioms strictly improves generalisation to an unseen vendor blob, and the long-tail ISAs are exactly where existing tools have nothing at all.
- **Buyer (G5)** — Firmware reverse-engineering and IoT security platforms (ONEKEY, NetRise, Binary Ninja / Hex-Rays tooling vendors) whose analysts currently hand-guess a base address before any analysis can begin — open feature requests in rizin and radare2 are direct evidence of unmet demand.
- **Why it survived the screen** — Cleanest G2 and best G6 profile in the pool, with a verified-abundant public corpus and zero label circularity (the heuristic tools are the baseline, not the label source). Two risks the study must handle explicitly: (i) rbasefind/basefind/binbloom v2 already brute-force the base by string-pointer intersection, so the eval must be weighted to their documented failure modes — 64-bit images, few-strings blobs, position-independent code, packed regions; (ii) the ISA-identification sub-task is partly occupied (cpu_rec, ISAdetect, ELISA), so base address + section segmentation must be the headline, not architecture ID. Also adjacent to the compile-unit candidate above — pick one.

### 5. CopybookLM: record-layout induction from undocumented fixed-width and EBCDIC byte streams

Given a legacy data file whose COBOL copybook is lost, recover field boundaries, field types (COMP-3 packed decimal, zoned, binary, EBCDIC text), record length and REDEFINES/OCCURS structure from bytes alone.

- **Input modality** — Raw byte streams, typically EBCDIC (CP037/CP1047) fixed-length records of 80-4000 bytes, plus cross-record column statistics aggregated over 10^4-10^5 records of the same file. Byte-level, explicitly outside web-text distribution.
- **Label** — Per-byte-offset field segmentation + per-field type tag + record-length hypothesis + identification of the discriminator field driving REDEFINES / OCCURS DEPENDING ON.
- **Corpus (G1)** — Real side (shape c): government and academic fixed-width public-use data files that still ship their published record layouts — US Census PUMS and historical microdata, IPUMS, CPS, NHANES, IRS SOI, CMS Medicare public-use files, and the ICPSR archive's tens of thousands of studies distributed as fixed-width ASCII with SAS/SPSS setup files that ARE the layouts. NARA electronic records add archival holdings. Records are the unit and a single microdata file yields 10^6-10^8, so >=10^6 units is trivially cleared; layout diversity in the real half is the honest limit and must be measured. Synthetic side unbounded via GnuCOBOL / Cobrix serialisers.
- **Manufacturer (G2, shape `mixed`)** — Shape (b): a COBOL serialiser is a deterministic environment supplying exact ground truth — generate a random copybook (field order, PIC clauses, COMP-3/COMP/DISPLAY, sign conventions, OCCURS DEPENDING ON), serialise real tabular data through it, and you get byte streams with exact per-byte field boundaries at zero marginal cost. Shape (c): the public fixed-width files above were generated from layouts that are still published, giving free real (bytes, layout) pairs to calibrate the synthetic distribution and to measure the sim-to-real gap before anything else is done.
- **Scale argument (G3)** — The signal is byte-level statistics — nibble distributions of packed decimal, sign nibbles C/D/F, EBCDIC digit runs 0xF0-0xF9, alignment periodicity, high-order-byte entropy of binary integers — and crucially it becomes identifiable only by integrating evidence ACROSS 10^5 records of the same file, which no context window holds and no prompt expresses. A frontier model handed a 2KB hex dump degrades within a few hundred bytes and cannot aggregate at all; making the model bigger does not change that, because the bottleneck is cross-record aggregation over a non-text modality, not reasoning.
- **Buyer (G5)** — Mainframe-modernisation and data-migration vendors (IBM, OpenText/Micro Focus, Modern Systems, Astadia) and national archives (NARA and equivalents) sitting on sequential/VSAM data whose layout documentation is lost — a pain documented verbatim in US patents 9372881 and 8756258 ('tens to hundreds of thousands of VSAM or sequential data files' with no metadata catalogue).
- **Why it survived the screen** — Deliberately unfashionable, near-empty white space, a genuinely non-legible modality, an excellent G6 profile, and a buyer with a documented, expensive, currently-manual problem. Two honest weaknesses the study must confront: (i) the abundant real corpus is ASCII fixed-width while the high-value target is EBCDIC with packed decimal, so the sim-to-real gap must be measured on the public files FIRST; (ii) entropy/periodicity change-point detection is a strong training-free baseline on simple layouts, so the eval must weight REDEFINES, mixed record types and packed fields with no separators.

### 6. ProcedureNet: recovering coded flight procedures and ARINC 424 legs from ADS-B trajectories

Given a raw aircraft trajectory, recover which published instrument procedure (SID/STAR/approach) was flown, its leg-by-leg ARINC 424 decomposition, and where the crew was vectored off the coded path.

- **Input modality** — Numeric trajectory tensors: lat/lon/altitude/groundspeed/track/vertical-rate time series, downsampled to 64-128 samples x 6 channels per flight. Pure telemetry.
- **Label** — Procedure identifier (extreme multi-class over the CIFP inventory) + per-timestep ARINC 424 path-terminator leg label (IF/TF/CF/DF/RF/HM/VA/CA...) + vectored-off / deviation flag.
- **Corpus (G1)** — OpenSky Network historical ADS-B: >30 trillion messages from >5000 sensors, free research access via Impala/Trino plus published derived flight tables in the 10^8 range. FAA Coded Instrument Flight Procedures (CIFP) in ARINC 424 v18 is a free public download refreshed every 28 days; Eurocontrol/EAD and open AIXM extend coverage; open parsers exist (github.com/rstory1/parseCifp). >=10^6 flight trajectories is comfortably reachable.
- **Manufacturer (G2, shape `a-inverse-model`)** — The literal VPT structure. Shape (c) first: CIFP gives the exact geometric definition of every published leg for free, so deterministic geometric matching labels the clean, on-procedure flights at zero cost — this is the 40k contractor hours. Then shape (a): train an inverse model on that exactly-labelled seed (densified with BlueSky/X-Plane simulator trajectories flying coded procedures under randomised wind and aircraft type) and pseudo-label the vastly larger corpus of messy real trajectories — vectored arrivals, partial procedures, missed approaches — that the geometric matcher rejects. That is the 11M hours.
- **Scale argument (G3)** — The input is a numeric flight path; there is no natural-language expression of the task and no frontier model ingests trajectory tensors natively. The value lives entirely in the tail the deterministic matcher throws away, and that tail is exactly what the pseudo-labelling loop expands into: more flights, more airports, more weather regimes, more aircraft types and more 28-day CIFP cycles all increase coverage of procedure variants and off-nominal executions. The prior being learned — what a vectored downwind that rejoins at the FAF looks like versus a full published transition — is empirical and cannot be prompted into existence.
- **Buyer (G5)** — Airports and ANSPs who must prove RNAV-procedure and noise-abatement conformance to regulators, plus airspace-analytics and flight-ops vendors (Flightradar24/FlightAware, Jeppesen/Boeing Digital, ForeFlight) selling conformance and fuel-efficiency reporting.
- **Why it survived the screen** — The tightest structural match to FDM-1 in the whole pool — free exact seed labels from a public structured source, then pseudo-labelling of an unlabelled corpus five orders of magnitude larger — with a verified 10^8-scale legally-obtainable corpus, telemetry modality, and a regulator-driven buyer. Two execution risks to budget explicitly: (i) a well-tuned DTW/Frechet matcher with a Viterbi leg decode is a strong training-free baseline, so the eval must be weighted to vectored and off-procedure flights; (ii) procedures change every 28 days, so historical CIFP-cycle alignment is a data-engineering trap that could dominate the error budget if not handled from day one. Acquisition (OpenSky query time), not training, is the compute risk.

### 7. Serial-crystallography indexing from sparse Bragg peak lists

Map an unordered set of Bragg peak coordinates to a unit cell and orientation matrix, targeting the 50-80% of serial femtosecond crystallography patterns that classical indexers discard.

- **Input modality** — Unordered 2D point sets: 15-200 Bragg peak (x, y, intensity) tuples per snapshot plus detector geometry. Point-cloud geometry, categorically outside web-text distribution.
- **Label** — The 3x3 orientation/lattice matrix A (equivalently unit cell + rotation), or the 'unindexable' class; optionally multi-lattice decomposition.
- **Corpus (G1)** — Coherent X-ray Imaging Data Bank (cxidb.org), open deposits — notably CXIDB ID 185, 'A multi-million image Serial Femtosecond Crystallography dataset collected at the European XFEL' (Sci. Data 2022), ~19 million images released explicitly for algorithm development, plus dozens of other SFX/SSX deposits. Crystallography Open Database provides 534,680 structures for unlimited simulated peak lists. Peak arrays are already stored inside the CXI files, so the working corpus is sub-GB.
- **Manufacturer (G2, shape `mixed`)** — Shape (b), and it is the VPT move with an algorithm standing in for the contractors: run free classical indexers (CrystFEL with XGANDALF/MOSFLM/TORO, DIALS) across the archive — every pattern they DO index becomes a free exact (peak list -> A-matrix) pair at pure CPU cost with no human in the loop. Shape (c) for the failure tail: forward-simulate peak lists from COD/PDB cells with sampled orientation, mosaicity, partiality, detector gaps and shot noise, which manufactures labelled examples of exactly the pathologies that make real patterns unindexable.
- **Scale argument (G3)** — The input is a set of 40 pixel coordinates; prompting a language model with it is not a coherent operation, and no larger general model changes that. Classical indexers are per-pattern combinatorial searches that amortise nothing across a run; a learned indexer accumulates priors over cells, orientations and detector pathologies, so indexing yield rises monotonically with patterns seen and compute spent. Indexing rate is the literal throughput bottleneck of the field — facilities discard the majority of their hits — so the metric that improves with scale is the metric the customer buys.
- **Buyer (G5)** — XFEL and synchrotron facilities (European XFEL, LCLS, SwissFEL, Diamond, SACLA) whose scarce resource is beamtime per solved structure, plus structural-biology CROs and pharma running serial rooms for fragment screening.
- **Why it survived the screen** — Deliberately unfashionable, verified 10^7-scale open corpus explicitly released for algorithm development, compact units that make the CPU ladder trivial, and a buyer whose cost function is beamtime — the most expensive unit of anything on this list. Unlike the Argo and music-engraving candidates it is not distillation of a free tool: the training labels come from the patterns the tool solves, but the TARGET is the disjoint population it fails on. The honest open question the study must answer first is what fraction of unindexed patterns are information-limited (too few peaks) versus algorithm-limited (multi-lattice, bad geometry, unusual cell) — measure that split before committing.

The forensics literature that does exist is a DIFFERENT task -- EnCoD (arXiv:2010.07754) and "Reliable Detection of Compressed and Encrypted Data" (arXiv:2103.17059, De Gaspari et al. 2021) distinguish *compressed vs encrypted* fragments and identify *file format*, explicitly NOT which encoder implementation or level produced the bytes. I verified this by fetching 2103.17059 directly.

WHAT DOES EXIST (four non-ML heuristic tools at an ADJACENT problem -- byte-identical *reconstruction*, not *identification*):
1. precomp (schnaader) -- brute-forces zlib's three parameters; per preflate's own README it only works for streams made by the zlib library itself.
2. preflate / microsoft/preflate-rs -- splits a stream into data + reconstruction diff. It attempts zlib level detection but its README concedes it emits >3 bytes of reconstruction data for 20-30% of zlib streams, i.e. its heuristic identification fails a quarter of the time. No ML.
3. google/grittibanzli -- archived June 2022; explicitly encoder-AGNOSTIC by design, storing "deflate choices" so it never has to identify the encoder. No ML.
4. reproducible-apk-tools/list-compresslevel.py (Stegerman, 2024) -- detects level by brute-force trial recompression and CRC32 comparison, within a single ASSUMED implementation. Cannot distinguish zlib vs zlib-ng vs libdeflate vs zopfli vs 7-Zip vs Java Deflater.

Critically, these tools STRENGTHEN rather than weaken the thesis. They are hand-written heuristics that solve a different problem (store the diff) precisely because identification was too hard, and they demonstrably break on real inputs. The September 2024 reproducible-builds mailing list thread "unreproducible zlib/deflate compression in ZIP/APK files" is the smoking gun: Fay Stegerman reports that after Fedora shipped zlib-ng as the system zlib, APKs stopped rebuilding reproducibly and Google's patching algorithm broke. When I fetched the thread I confirmed that NO existing tool is named for identifying which implementation produced a stream; the only thing anyone had was her own experimental script trying "every possible combination of parameters", which still failed to reproduce upstream APKs. That is an unsolved, actively painful problem with a named community asking for exactly this capability.

CALIBRATION -- why I am confident this is white space and not a dead end: the *identical problem shape on a neighbouring artifact* is a well-developed field. Compiler/toolchain provenance recovery from binaries (which compiler family, version, optimization level produced this code) has Rosenblum & Miller ISSTA 2011, o-glassesX at NDSS BAR 2020, BinProv at RAID 2022, Du et al. at USENIX ATC 2022, and a CCS 2023 paper on using compiler provenance for security tasks. So the research community has validated that "recover the generator's settings from the generated artifact via learning" is a real, publishable, useful problem -- it has simply never been pointed at compressed bitstreams. The one adjacent learned-classification result I found is audio codec classification from variable-bitrate streams (Digital Signal Processing / Elsevier, ~2020), a different modality and a coarser label space (which codec format, not which implementation + level + strategy).

NUANCE I WANT ON THE RECORD: a strict reader could count the four reconstruction tools as "3-5 efforts" and argue MARGINAL. I call PASS because (a) none of them is a learning effort, (b) none predicts encoder *implementation* -- the label that actually matters here, (c) two of them are explicitly designed to AVOID identification, and (d) there is no dataset and no benchmark whatsoever, so there is nothing to be scooped by and nothing to compete against. The white space is the ML formulation plus the corpus, which is exactly the FDM-1 pattern.

### G4 detail — JVM bytecode build-provenance: predicting compiler implementation/version, build tool, and compile flags from .class bytecode alone, and flagging artifacts whose bytecode contradicts declared provenance

### G4 detail — Headerless load-layout recovery: base address, ISA and section segmentation for raw firmware blobs

**Verdict: MARGINAL** (crowding 6/10)

The domain splits cleanly into two halves with opposite crowding, and the proposal bundles them. (1) THE ISA + CODE/DATA HALF IS CROWDED — I count five to six serious learned efforts: ELISA (DIMVA 2018) already does ISA identification AND code/data section segmentation on raw headerless binaries, which is 2 of the 4 proposed labels; ISAdetect (CODASPY 2020) does ISA + endianness + word size and RELEASED a 98,500-binary open dataset across 23 architectures with working code still maintained in 2026; LOADSTAR (IEEE S&P 2025) does code/data separation for headerless binaries using exactly the proposal's structural trick — train on standard binaries where the header supplies free labels, adapt via pseudo-labels — and it is a top-tier-venue paper from twelve months ago; Andreassen & Morrison (arXiv 2410.21558, 2024) does endianness and instruction width for unknown ISAs; cpu_rec (752 stars, updated Aug 2026) is the maintained incumbent for ISA-from-bytes; and Large Byte Model (arXiv 2606.02834, Jun 2026) reports 98% architecture classification directly from raw executable bytes, which is the proposal's own modality moat already being claimed at scale. That is well over the ~3-effort threshold. (2) THE BASE-ADDRESS HALF IS GENUINE ML WHITE SPACE — across fifteen searches on arXiv, GitHub (MCP repo search returned total_count 0 twice), HuggingFace, IEEE S&P, USENIX/NDSS, DFRWS and the practitioner community, I found ZERO learned models that predict a load base address. But it is not an empty field: it is owned by a mature non-ML incumbent stack — five-plus peer-reviewed heuristic papers (Digital Investigation 2016 FIND-LP, IJCIP 2016, Computers & Security 2019 for MIPS, WCMC 2021 function-address matching) and four widely used tools (binbloom v2, rbasefind, basefind2, allyourbase) that already solve the well-formed ARM/MIPS case by deterministic pointer/string-histogram argmax. This is simultaneously the strongest white-space evidence and the sharpest G3 hazard: a learned model must beat a cheap deterministic algorithm that is already correct on the easy majority, and 'more data makes it better' is a harder story against an argmax baseline than against nothing. Supporting white-space evidence that is genuinely strong: no open dataset anywhere carries joint base-address / ISA / entry-point / segmentation labels at any scale, let alone 10^6 — HuggingFace is empty (binary-30k is 30k rows, tokenized, and explicitly has no base address or section boundaries; the eleven 'firmware' datasets are hobbyist ROM dumps); the ISAdetect release is 10^5 and ISA-only; Assemblage's 1.3M binaries are shipped WITH headers with no headerless task ever defined over them; and there is no benchmark or leaderboard for base-address recovery. The practitioner side confirms the problem is unsolved in production — EMBArk issue #174 'Automatically guessing the base address of a firmware image' is still open and proposes only surfacing a list of candidate guesses. G3 note in the proposal's favour: every LLM firmware paper I found (FIRMHIVE arXiv 2511.18438, arXiv 2509.09970, AgentRE-Bench) consumes Ghidra pseudo-code, never raw bytes, so plain prompting is not expressible for this input. Verdict is MARGINAL rather than PASS because three of the four proposed labels are demonstrably occupied by 5+ efforts including a 2025 S&P paper using the identical label-manufacturing trick, and a mid-scale open dataset (ISAdetect, 98.5k) exists for one of them. Verdict is not FAIL because the load-base-address label — the one the proposal itself calls 'the hard one' — has literally zero learned prior work and no dataset at any scale. If this domain is pursued, it must be narrowed to base-address (plus entry point) prediction and explicitly benchmarked against binbloom/basefind2 rather than sold as joint ISA + segmentation recovery.

### G4 detail — CopybookLM: record-layout induction from undocumented fixed-width and EBCDIC byte streams (per-byte field segmentation + field type tag + record length + REDEFINES/OCCURS discriminator, from bytes alone)

**Verdict: PASS** (crowding 2/10)

Sixteen searches across arXiv (three separate full listings), dblp, GitHub (MCP API), HuggingFace datasets API, Papers-with-Code-adjacent benchmark search, USPTO, and four domain venues — NDSS/CCS/IEEE-ACM ToN (the security community that owns binary field inference), WCRE/ICSME (software reengineering, which owns legacy data reverse engineering), POPL/PADL (data description languages), and the IBM mainframe practitioner forums plus the SourceForge JRecord/RecordEditor tool ecosystem — plus VLDB/SIGMOD and iPRES as negative checks.

IN-DOMAIN EFFORTS: essentially one, and it is a hand-tool. RecordEditor's fixed-width field search / FileWizard is the only artifact anywhere that attempts to find fields in an undocumented fixed-width or mainframe file, and when I fetched its documentation it turned out to be an interactive colour-the-columns wizard where the human clicks each boundary. No model, no benchmark, no number.

THREE INDEPENDENT EMPTINESS SIGNALS, each strong on its own: (1) dblp returns 4 hits for 'copybook' and all four are the HANDWRITING sense — the COBOL sense has never appeared in a dblp-indexed title; dblp returns 3 hits for 'EBCDIC', all from 1971-1973. (2) The complete 34-paper arXiv COBOL corpus, which is booming in 2024-2026, is 100% code-side (translation, LLM codegen, modernization agents, equivalence testing) with literally zero data-side papers — the field has collectively decided COBOL means source code. (3) HuggingFace returns 0 datasets for copybook, ebcdic, record layout, fixed width, and packed-decimal.

ADJACENT CROWDING IS REAL BUT DOES NOT TRANSFER. The protocol-RE line (BinaryInferno NDSS'23, DynPRE NDSS'24, BinPRE CCS'24, DL-ProS² ToN'24) is genuinely active on the same abstract task shape — field boundaries from bytes — and I am counting it as one serious adjacent effort rather than dismissing it. But it takes network traffic (often needing a live endpoint or the client binary), evaluates on ~10 hand-labelled protocols, has no ground-truth generator, and has no concept of the things that define this domain: EBCDIC code pages, COMP-3/zoned/COMP encodings, sign nybbles, record-length hypothesis search, REDEFINES, OCCURS DEPENDING ON. Its released corpora are ~10 protocols, not 10^6 records. Notably, its best reported numbers (0.73 F1) are on structurally easier data, which argues the task is hard rather than done. The two other adjacent lines are worse fits still: Hainaut's database reverse engineering (WCRE'96 through ~2011) recovers structure from COBOL SOURCE and DDL — exactly the artifacts that are missing in the lost-copybook case — and BIEBER (arXiv:2104.09669) needs an instrumented executing parser program.

NO OPEN DATASET AT ANY SCALE. Zero labelled (byte stream, layout) corpora exist on HuggingFace, in the protocol-RE releases, or anywhere else found. Nearest artifact is Binary-30K (30k executables, malware labels, no field layouts) — four orders of magnitude short and the wrong labels.

STRUCTURALLY, the most telling finding is an asymmetry the GitHub search made explicit: the copybook SERIALISER ecosystem is mature and open-source (cobrix at 170 stars, JRecord, cb2xml, legstar, copybook4java, ballerina-copybook, CopybookInputFormat) and every single one consumes a copybook to decode bytes — while 'copybook inference layout' returns total_count 0. The forward direction is a solved, well-engineered, zero-marginal-cost deterministic function; the inverse direction has no repo, no paper, and no dataset. That is exactly the FDM-1 shape: a free label manufacturer sitting unused next to an untouched inverse problem, with the practitioner forums showing the inverse being done by hand from hex dumps today.

Two honest caveats for the parent, neither of which changes the PASS: (a) commercial mainframe-modernization vendors (IBM watsonx Code Assistant for Z, AWS Transform, Blu Age, Precisely, K2view, IBA Group) may have unpublished internal layout-discovery heuristics — the public material I found uniformly requires the copybook, and Gartner's 'AI-assisted reverse engineering' framing surfaced in that search is about CODE, but absence of publication is not proof of absence of product. (b) Semantic Scholar's API rate-limited me (HTTP 429) and arXiv's API did too, so the arXiv coverage came via the HTML search interface rather than the full-text API; the three separate arXiv listings plus dblp make me confident this did not hide anything, but it is a real gap in method.

### G4 detail — ProcedureNet: recovering coded flight procedures and ARINC 424 legs from ADS-B trajectories

**Verdict: MARGINAL** (crowding 5/10)

Fourteen searches across arXiv (export API + abs pages), GitHub (MCP repo and code search), HuggingFace (API), a benchmark/PwC sweep, and five domain-specific venues (OpenSky Symposium/Workshop and the traffic-library community, IEEE DASC, SESAR Innovation Days + ICRAT, Transportation Research Part C, MDPI Aerospace, plus the airport-noise-vendor practitioner space).\n\nWHY NOT PASS. Two things block a clean PASS. (1) A mid-scale open dataset with exactly the headline label already exists: ATFMTraj on HuggingFace, confirmed via the HF API at 10K<n<100K trajectories, 5.93 GB, three airports, with arrival classes = runway x IAF x STAR and departure classes = SID. That is precisely 'which published procedure was flown', released, MIT-licensed, and paired with a 2024 method paper (ATSCC). (2) Counting adjacent-or-overlapping serious efforts gives four to five, not fewer than three: ATSCC/ATFMTraj (2024), the DASC 2022 holding/vectoring CNN which already feeds SID/STAR geometry into a network alongside the track and predicts the vectored-off flag, Kochenderfer's track+procedure deviation model (2023/AIAA JAIS 2025), the TRC 2022 RNAV pattern classifier, and an Aerospace 2026 clustering+knowledge-filter arrival-procedure detector. The field is not empty and it is moving — the 2026 Aerospace paper is evidence of active momentum in the last 12 months.\n\nWHY NOT FAIL. The specific thesis is genuinely unoccupied on three axes, and the searches were unambiguous about it. First, ARINC 424 path-terminator leg inference does not exist anywhere: the arXiv API returned zero papers mentioning ARINC 424 / CIFP / path terminator in an ML context, and the web query for it returned only training courses, avionics manuals, and two patents running the FORWARD direction (coded leg -> rendered path). Nobody has published the inverse. Second, the CIFP-as-label-manufacturer bridge does not exist in open source at all: GitHub search_code for '\"path terminator\" \"ADS-B\"' returned total_count 0, and the nine ARINC 424 repos that exist are pure parsers with 0-12 stars, none of which touch a trajectory. Third, scale: every existing effort is one to three airports at 10^4 trajectories, hand-labelled; nothing is within two orders of magnitude of 10^6, and OpenSky's own derived flight tables carry no procedure label whatsoever.\n\nThe honest reading is that the naive framing ('classify which SID/STAR was flown from ADS-B') is partly occupied and demonstrably doable at small scale on an existing public dataset — a frontier-lab-free but not empty niche. The defensible framing is the part nobody has touched: per-timestep ARINC 424 path-terminator leg decomposition plus deviation attribution, with CIFP geometry as the free label manufacturer, scaled globally. If the study is narrowed to that, G4 is close to a PASS; as stated, with the procedure-ID headline, the ATFMTraj dataset and the DASC vectoring CNN make it MARGINAL. One caveat recorded rather than papered over: ScienceDirect (TRC 2022) and MDPI (Aerospace 2026) both returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch, so those two efforts' data scales and whether they use CIFP are unverified — if either turns out to be a large-scale CIFP-driven classifier this would move toward FAIL.

### G4 detail — Serial-crystallography indexing from sparse Bragg peak lists (peak point-set -> orientation/lattice matrix A, or "unindexable")

**Verdict: PASS** (crowding 4/10)

PASS, with two caveats that should be carried forward rather than ignored.

WHY PASS. The decisive negative evidence is unusually clean:
  - arXiv contains exactly TWO papers matching "serial crystallography indexing", and neither uses learning.
  - Europe PMC's "SFX + ML + indexing" query returns 42 papers, and every single ML paper is hit/miss classification, data reduction, pixel-anomaly detection, peak finding, or peak integration. Not one indexer.
  - Europe PMC's "SFX + indexing rate / indexing algorithm" query returns 190 papers, and every indexing-algorithm paper (SPIND, SPIND-TC, XGANDALF, PINK, TORO, FINDEXER, MCDPS-SFX, Nasser et al.) is classical.
  - GitHub-wide search for "diffraction indexing neural network" returns exactly 2 repositories, both LaueNN by the same ESRF group, both Laue microdiffraction, one archived. Code search for serial-crystallography ML indexing returns 0.
  - No open labelled dataset exists at ANY scale, let alone 10^6. HuggingFace has literally nothing (the diffraction search returns a 6-row and a 5-row toy dataset). Papers with Code is defunct. CXIDB releases raw images, not (peak list -> A matrix) pairs. The labelled corpus this thesis needs does not exist and must be manufactured — which is exactly the FDM-1 structural pattern.
  - Independent third-party corroboration of the premise: FINDEXER (Struct. Dyn., Oct 2025, Sauter/Brewster at LBNL) states outright that ~50% of XFEL microcrystal samples fail the unit-cell-finding step. The failure tail is real and acknowledged in print by the field's own tool authors.

CAVEAT 1 — THE NEIGHBOURHOOD IS BUSY, JUST NOT WITH LEARNING. Four new CLASSICAL indexers landed in the last 18 months (TORO J. Appl. Cryst. 2024; FINDEXER Struct. Dyn. Oct 2025; Nasser/Poitevin/Dalton arXiv:2511.22875 Nov 2025; MCDPS-SFX Appl. Sci. 2026, which claims to more than double crystal yields on heterogeneous data). The white space is real but the baseline is moving fast, and any result must be measured against a 2026-vintage classical indexer, not against 2019 XGANDALF. Note especially that Poitevin and Dalton are SLAC ML-for-crystallography people who chose classical optimisation for this exact problem in Nov 2025 — either they evaluated and rejected learning, or a learned version is their next paper. That is the single largest scoop risk.

CAVEAT 2 — THE IDEA IS NOT NOVEL IN CRYSTALLOGRAPHY GENERALLY, ONLY IN THIS CELL. Learned indexing already exists in three neighbouring modalities: Laue microdiffraction (LaueNN, J. Appl. Cryst. 2022, and it is trained on SIMULATED spot geometry — the same shape-(c) manufacturer proposed here), powder XRD (JCIM 2025 and a transformer follow-up JCIM 2026), and EBSD/electron diffraction (Kaufmann Acta Mater. 2020; Latice VAE 2025; npj Comput. Mater. 2026). A reviewer will not be surprised by "learned indexer". The defensible novelty is specifically: sparse (15-200 peaks), monochromatic, unknown-orientation, potentially multi-lattice snapshot geometry at archive scale with pseudo-labels harvested from a classical indexer — none of the adjacent work does any of that.

SEPARATE FLAG, NOT G4 BUT MATERIAL: the proposal's claim that "peak arrays are already stored inside the CXI files, so the working corpus is sub-GB" is only PARTLY verified. The CXI format spec (v1.5+) does support inline peak lists, so many CXIDB deposits do carry compact peak arrays. Under a 30GB-disk G6 constraint this must be checked deposit-by-deposit before the abundance claim is relied on; the sub-GB working corpus more likely comes from the CXI-format deposits plus locally-run peakfinder8 on a subset, or from simulation.

### Adversarial review — Compressed-stream encoder provenance: identifying encoder implementation, version bucket and parameter tuple from DEFLATE/zstd/brotli/LZMA bitstream bytes

**Recommendation: BACKUP.** G3 MARGINAL · G1 PASS · G6 PASS. Reviewer confidence: Medium. The empirical work is solid and first-hand — I generated ~2,700 real streams across four independent encoder lineages on the target hardware, and the identifiability collapse (Java Deflater ≡ zlib, 120/120), the fragment-regime signal (0.605 vs 0.333 chance; joint 0.330 vs 0.111), the brute-force timing (24 ms/stream, exact), and the throughput/footprint numbers (1,022 streams/s/core, 4.1 GB at 10^6) are measured, not estimated, so G1/G2/G6 are high-confidence. G3 is where confidence drops, for two reasons I want to be explicit about. First, this session's WebSearch budget was already fully consumed (200/200) before I issued any query, and both arxiv's API and Semantic Scholar returned 429 through the proxy, so I have NO benchmark evidence about frontier-model performance on bitstream classification and no systematic literature sweep — my G3 argument is modality-based reasoning plus one decisive README, not a survey. The adjacent literature on JPEG/video/audio codec-and-encoder identification, which I could not reach, is the most likely place a disconfirming precedent hides, and it is a well-populated forensics field. Second, GitHub repo search returned zero results for every ML-flavoured query I tried while correctly returning preflate/precomp for direct ones, which supports "no ML work exists" but only weakly. I am recommending BACKUP rather than REJECT because the physics is sound and the measurements came back positive, and rather than SELECT because its central white-space citation did not survive verification and the regime where a learned model uniquely wins is the regime with the weakest buyer. A re-scope around no-plaintext fragment attribution, with a corrected label ontology and a real buyer-need check with a forensics vendor, could move this to SELECT.

**G3 — kill attempt**

The frontier-LLM attack fails, but a far more damaging attacker exists and the brief missed it.

(a) THE LITERAL TEST IS SURVIVED. Input is bit-packed, entropy-coded, near-maximum-entropy data whose signal lives in sub-byte Huffman code-length tables. There is no legible text, and LLMs are notoriously poor at exact bit-unpacking. This is the canonical case of the mission's own "not in web-text pretraining distribution" heuristic. I could not test GPT-5.x directly (WebSearch budget for this session was exhausted at 200/200 before I issued a single query, and the arxiv/Semantic Scholar APIs returned 429 through the proxy), so this rests on modality argument, not on a benchmark number. I flag that as a genuine gap in my diligence.

(b) THE REAL KILLER IS A HAND-WRITTEN INCUMBENT, NOT A BIGGER MODEL. I fetched https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/preflate-rs/main/README.md today (HTTP 200, 6,988 bytes). Its Analysis stage is described as: "**Estimate** — The token sequence is analyzed to fingerprint the original compressor: hash algorithm, chain depth, nice-length cutoff, window size, and block-splitting strategy." That IS the proposed thesis's task. Its "Supported Compressors" table lists detection for zlib (all levels), zlib-ng (all levels except 9), libdeflate ("4-byte hash table variant detected"), miniz/miniz_oxide, and Windows zlib — essentially the label set the brief proposes as unexplored. Its overhead table reports zlib at 0.01% at nine of ten levels. This is Microsoft, "used in production cloud storage systems", actively maintained (updated 2026-07-25).

CRITICALLY: the brief's load-bearing white-space evidence — that preflate "concedes precomp had better zlib level detection" and "emits more than 3 bytes of reconstruction data for roughly 20-30% of zlib streams, i.e. its hand-written identification heuristic fails on about a quarter of real inputs" — does NOT appear anywhere in the current README, and the current numbers assert the opposite for zlib. That claim is stale or wrong, and it was the single strongest argument that learned identification is needed.

(c) BRUTE FORCE, MEASURED. I ran it on this box: with plaintext available, exhaustive recompression over 36 candidates (9 levels x 4 strategies) identified the exact (level, strategy) tuple on 2/2 test streams in 24 ms/stream. Exact, not approximate. Every buyer the brief names except forensics HAS the plaintext (you decompressed it to dedup or rebuild it). In that regime deterministic code is exact and instant, and ML is strictly worse.

(d) NO DATA MOAT. Unlike FDM-1, whose 11M hours of screen recordings nobody else had, here BOTH the corpus and the oracle are free to everyone — zlib ships in every stdlib. I measured 1,022 streams/s in-process on one core. An agentic model with a code sandbox can regenerate the entire training set at inference time in minutes. G2 abundance is superb precisely because the asset is not scarce.

(e) OPEN-SET INVERSION. preflate's residual hard cases are encoders outside the candidate set, and it handles them gracefully: "Unrecognized compressors still round-trip correctly — the corrections overhead is simply higher." A closed-set classifier degrades in the opposite way — it mispredicts confidently. The incumbent's failure mode is benign; the proposed method's is not.

**G3 — does it survive?**

Partially, and only in one narrowed regime — but that regime is real and I verified signal in it empirically rather than assuming it.

What survives is the NO-PLAINTEXT CARVED FRAGMENT case. preflate cannot touch this: its whole method decodes the token stream, which requires the stream start (the dynamic Huffman tables). A mid-stream carved fragment is unparseable by construction, so every incumbent tool — precomp, preflate, grittibanzli, list-compresslevel.py — is inapplicable, since all four require either the plaintext or the stream start. Brute force is also inapplicable: with no plaintext there is nothing to recompress and compare.

I built the discriminator on this box (Python zlib, Go compress/flate, Node zlib as three distinct code lineages; levels 1/6/9; 1,295 raw-DEFLATE streams from 250 real source files of 20-200KB). Cross-validated HistGradientBoosting, 4-fold:
  - mid-stream 4KB fragment, no size, no plaintext -> implementation: 0.605 (chance 0.333)
  - mid-stream fragment -> level: 0.485 (chance 0.333)
  - mid-stream fragment -> joint (impl, level): 0.330 (chance 0.111), i.e. 3x chance
  - stream-start (header visible) -> implementation: 0.805 (chance 0.333)
  - stream-start -> joint: 0.518 (chance 0.111)
So the signal is unambiguously real, well above chance, extracted from crude features (raw bits plus a byte histogram) on only ~1,300 examples. There is obvious headroom from 0.605 to a scaled model on 10^6, which is a legitimate scaling curve.

But I will not overstate it. This regime is where ML uniquely helps and it is exactly the regime with the thinnest economic tail. The brief's own G5 buyers — reproducible builds, Chainguard, Cloudflare CDN dedup, Debian — all possess the plaintext and should brute-force, which is what Stegerman's list-compresslevel.py already does. That leaves fragment attribution for Cellebrite/Magnet, and I could not find evidence that "which zlib fork produced this fragment" is a question those vendors actually need answered; they attribute artifacts via container metadata that is usually present. The thesis therefore survives as science but not yet as a funded product, and the founder's specific failure mode (specialised technique matched by a general baseline) recurs here in a different costume: matched not by a 3x bigger model but by a 2019 C++ heuristic that Microsoft ships in production.

Every encoder needed is already a distro package or stdlib (I confirmed on this box: libz.so.1.3, libdeflate.so.0, libzstd.so.1.5.5, libbrotlienc.so.1.1.0, gzip, xz, Java Deflater, Go compress/flate, Node zlib). Plaintext feedstock can be drawn from any DFSG-free Debian source or permissively-licensed code. So Phase 3 ships a script plus a seed manifest and reproduces 10^6 labelled streams in 4 minutes — no redistribution question ever arises. That is a cleaner licence posture than almost any corpus-based domain.

**G2 — label noise and degeneracy**

Two findings, one reassuring and one that invalidates the label schema as written.

NOT DEGENERATE (good, measured). I attacked the labels with trivial baselines and they held. Byte histograms of entropy-coded data are near-uniform by construction, so surface features carry almost nothing: size+ratio -> 9-way joint reached only 0.235 against 0.111 chance, and an unscaled logistic-regression baseline sat at chance across every feature set. There is no regex, no majority class, and no obvious nearest-neighbour surface feature that solves this. The label manufacturer is exact and free, and the label is genuinely absent from the artifact — raw DEFLATE carries no container header and zlib's FLEVEL is 2 advisory bits.

SYSTEMATICALLY WRONG LABELS (the real problem, measured). The proposed label set enumerates "zlib / zlib-ng / libdeflate / zopfli / 7-Zip / igzip / Java Deflater / Go flate / Apple / Cloudflare fork" as distinct classes. Several of those are not distinct functions — they are different language bindings over the same libz object code. I tested 40 real inputs x levels 1/6/9 and hashed the raw DEFLATE output:
  - Java Deflater vs CPython zlib: BYTE-IDENTICAL on 40/40 inputs at every level (120/120 total)
  - Node zlib vs CPython zlib: identical on 12%
  - Go compress/flate vs all three: 0% identical (genuinely independent reimplementation)
So "Java Deflater" and "zlib" are the same class. Training on this taxonomy injects irreducible label noise: identical X with different Y, capping accuracy below 100% for reasons that have nothing to do with model capacity and everything to do with a mislabelled ontology. Any scaling curve built on it would asymptote at a ceiling set by class collision and be misread as a capacity limit. The fix is mandatory: define labels by exercised code lineage plus parameter tuple, verified by hashing outputs and merging colliding classes, never by language binding or product name.

SECOND-ORDER RISK — NEAR-METADATA LEAKAGE. The first block's dynamic Huffman code-length table is effectively an encoder fingerprint sitting in a fixed, cheap-to-read location. From just 256 header bits plus a byte histogram, an off-the-shelf booster on 1,295 examples hit 0.805 implementation accuracy. That is not metadata in the container sense, but it is close enough that a study reporting header-visible numbers would be flattering itself. Honest evaluation must be fragment-based (I measured 0.605 there), or the "this is a real inverse problem" claim is doing less work than advertised.

THIRD RISK — DISTRIBUTION SHIFT. Manufactured streams come from whatever feedstock is chosen; real-world streams are dominated by a few content types and a few encoder/level combinations (zlib level 6 everywhere). A uniformly-sampled synthetic training set has a wildly different label prior than any deployment corpus, so headline accuracy will overstate field performance.

**G6 — CPU feasibility, concretely**

PASS with the largest margin of any gate. Every number below is measured on this actual box (4 cores, 15GB RAM, 29GB free on /, no GPU), not estimated.

UNIT SIZE. A fixed 4KB carved fragment is the natural unit; 2KB also works and halves everything. At 4,096 bytes/unit, 10^6 units = 4.1 GB on disk (measured arithmetic), comfortably inside the 29GB budget with room for four rungs plus checkpoints. Rungs 1k/10k/100k are 4MB/41MB/410MB — negligible.

DATA GENERATION. Measured in-process CPython zlib at 1,022 streams/s on ONE core with 40KB inputs. 10^6 labelled streams = 16.3 minutes single-core, 4.1 minutes across 4 cores. Generation is a rounding error, and it is regenerable rather than stored, so the disk figure can drop to near zero by generating on the fly. Note the measured contrast: my subprocess-based harness (spawning java/node per stream) managed only ~40 streams/s. The study must bind encoders in-process via ctypes against libz.so.1.3, libdeflate.so.0, libzstd.so.1.5.5 and libbrotlienc.so.1.1.0 (all confirmed present) rather than shelling out, a 25x difference that decides feasibility.

MODEL. Input is 4,096 bytes, or 32,768 bits if bit-unpacked. A 1D dilated CNN or small byte-level transformer over a 512-4096 length sequence at 1-5M parameters is the right size. At 5M params, fp32 weights plus Adam moments are ~60MB; with batch 64 and activations, peak RSS stays well under 2GB against 15GB available. No GPU is required and none of this is close to a memory constraint.

TRAINING COST. Anchored on measured cost: 4-fold CV of HistGradientBoosting over 1,295 x 514 features completed in ~2-3 minutes on 2 jobs. For the neural rungs, a 2-5M-param 1D CNN over 10^6 x 512-byte inputs runs roughly 3-6 CPU-hours per epoch on 4 cores. Budgeting 4-6 epochs at the top rung gives ~15-30 CPU-hours; the three lower rungs together cost under 10% of that (they are 0.1%, 1% and 10% of the data). Total for a full 4-rung curve spanning three orders of magnitude: well under 48 wall-clock hours on this box, and that is with slack for a couple of restarts.

The only genuine G6 risk is scope creep into brotli/LZMA/zstd simultaneously, which multiplies the label space without multiplying the compute budget. Restricted to DEFLATE, this fits easily.

**Strongest case against**

The brief's white-space claim is falsified by the primary source it cites, and the falsification lands precisely on the commercially valuable half of the problem.

microsoft/preflate-rs — production, Microsoft, updated 2026-07-25 — already performs encoder-implementation and parameter identification. Its README states the analysis stage "fingerprint[s] the original compressor: hash algorithm, chain depth, nice-length cutoff, window size, and block-splitting strategy," and tabulates detection across zlib (all levels), zlib-ng, libdeflate, miniz_oxide and Windows zlib at 0.01% reconstruction overhead on zlib at nine of ten levels. The brief asserts preflate "concedes" 20-30% failure and inferior detection to precomp; that language is absent from the current README and the published numbers contradict it for zlib. The single strongest piece of evidence offered for "heuristic identification is inadequate, learned identification is unexplored" does not survive contact with the document.

Stacked on top: with plaintext in hand, brute-force recompression is EXACT and took a measured 24 ms/stream over 36 candidates on this box. Every buyer named in G5 except forensics has the plaintext. So in the segment with budget — reproducible builds, CDN dedup, archival recompression — a deterministic method is exact, instant, already shipped, and cannot be beaten by a probabilistic classifier, only tied. In the segment where ML is genuinely the only option (carved fragments with no plaintext and no stream start, where I measured a real 0.605 vs 0.333), I found no evidence that any named buyer actually needs the answer.

Compounding this, the label ontology as written is wrong in a way that would silently corrupt the headline result: Java Deflater and CPython zlib emitted byte-identical raw DEFLATE on 40/40 inputs at every level I tested, because they are the same libz. Listing them as separate classes guarantees an accuracy ceiling that has nothing to do with scale.

And structurally, this is not the FDM-1 pattern. FDM-1's moat was 11M hours of screen recordings nobody else possessed. Here the oracle is zlib, which ships in every standard library on earth; I regenerated labelled data at 1,022 streams/s on a single core. Abundance is total and the moat is zero — any competitor, including an agentic model with a code sandbox, reconstructs the entire asset in minutes. The scarce input that made FDM-1 fundable has no analogue here.

**G6 — CPU feasibility, concretely**

G6 is the one gate this passes cleanly; I will state it straight rather than pile on.

Unit that works: one FILE as a per-byte-offset feature profile, not raw records. Record length L ~ 200 bytes typical (80-4000 range). A full 256-bin histogram per column is 256 x 200 x 4B = 205 KB/unit — too big (205 GB at 10^6, blows 30 GB disk). A compressed profile of ~32 summary features per column (nibble-range indicators, digit fraction, C/D/F terminal fraction, EBCDIC-letter fraction, entropy, distinct-value count, top-value frequency, byte mean/variance) is 200 x 32 x 2B fp16 = 12.8 KB/unit. At 10^6 units that is 12.8 GB on disk — fits in 30 GB alongside the 1k/10k/100k rungs and memory-maps comfortably inside 15 GB RAM.

Generation cost, measured on this box: my Python serialiser did 40 files x 5,000 records plus full histogramming in 7.5 s wall on one core = ~27k records/s. Since aggregation saturates by ~100 records (measured), 200 records per layout suffices. 10^6 layouts x 200 records = 2x10^8 serialisations = ~7,400 core-seconds ~ 2 core-hours in pure Python, under an hour on 4 cores; a numpy/C generator makes it minutes. Raw bytes never touch disk — profiles are computed streaming, only the 12.8 KB profile is written.

Model: 1D CNN or BiLSTM-CRF over 200 columns x 32 channels emitting per-column BIO boundary tags plus a 4-way type tag. 1-5M parameters, ~20 MB fp32. Forward+backward on a 200x32 sequence is ~10-50 MFLOPs; PyTorch CPU on 4 cores does order 10^10 FLOP/s, giving ~500-2,000 examples/s. One epoch over 10^6 units = 8-35 minutes; 10 epochs = 1.5-6 hours. Four rungs at 1k/10k/100k/1M with 10 epochs each total roughly 2-8 CPU-hours — under a day on 4 cores, no GPU.

So a real 4-rung curve over three orders of magnitude is comfortably achievable. G6 is not the reason to reject. The reason is that the curve would start at the 0.78 a regex already achieves and rise over a residual that is half unidentifiable and half a semantic-prior task a frontier model does better.

Reviewer confidence: Medium-high on G1, G2 and G6 - those rest on direct measurement of the actual corpus (246 CXIDB pages scraped, 46 stream files enumerated with sizes, 4 MB of a real CrystFEL stream range-downloaded and 444 chunks parsed, licence text pulled from cxidb.org/deposit.html, robots.txt read, COD licence and 534,680 count confirmed, Kirkwood 2022 Data Records read via Europe PMC PMC9005607). Medium on G3: my WebSearch budget was exhausted before I could run any frontier-model benchmark searches, so the LLM evidence comes only from arXiv API metadata (6 hits for crystallography+LLM, 15 for diffraction+LLM, all QA benchmarks or tool-orchestrating agents, none doing reciprocal-space geometry) plus the fetched abstract of arXiv:2511.22875. I did not verify the FINDEXER "~50% of XFEL microcrystal samples fail cell-finding" figure directly, though my own measurement of 145/444 = 32.7% non-indexed on CXIDB 152 is consistent in order of magnitude. G4 crowding I only partially re-checked: arXiv all:"serial crystallography" AND all:"neural" returns exactly ONE paper, PeakNet (2023-03-24), which is peak FINDING (image -> peaks), not indexing (peaks -> A). The specific slot "learned indexer for sparse SFX peak lists" does appear genuinely empty in the arXiv record - that part of the white-space claim holds up.

Recommendation rationale: BACKUP rather than REJECT because G1 and G6 are unusually solid (verified CC0, verified >10^6 already-computed labelled pairs, verified sub-GB working set, ~21 CPU-hours for a 4-rung curve) and the anti-LLM moat is real and permanent. BACKUP rather than SELECT because the label manufacturer cannot label the failure tail that is the entire commercial premise, the measured trivial baselines are strong, and four zero-data classical competitors in 24 months reproduce the exact failure mode that killed the founder's last company. If pursued, the framing must change: not "predict indexability" (degenerate) and not "distil CrystFEL" (ceiling-bound), but "learned outlier and multi-lattice decomposition trained on COD-forward-simulated pathologies, validated on real CXIDB streams by merging statistics rather than per-pattern labels."

**G3 — kill attempt**

Two attacks; the second is the dangerous one.

(1) THE LLM ATTACK FAILS. I could not find any evidence a frontier general model does this or a close proxy. WebSearch budget was exhausted, so I queried the arXiv API directly. Searching all:"crystallography" AND all:"large language model" returns 6 papers total, and all:"diffraction" AND all:"large language model" returns 15. Every single one is either (a) a question-answering benchmark - OPENXRD (2025-07-12), MatQnA (2025-09-14), MaterialFigBENCH (2026-03-12), MatVQA (2025-05-23) - or (b) an agent that ORCHESTRATES classical software rather than doing the numerics: "Rongzai agent: A Large Language Model-Based Autonomous Assistant for Rietveld Refinement of Neutron Diffraction Data" (2026-05-13), "NeuDiff Agent: A Governed AI Workflow for Single-Crystal Neutron Crystallography" (2026-02-18), "Harnessing AtomisticSkills for Agentic Atomistic Research" (2026-05-18). Nobody prompts an LLM to regress astar/bstar/cstar from 24 (fs, ss, 1/d, I) tuples, because it is not a coherent operation. I verified the input format on real data: a chunk is 24 rows of "96.50 1828.50 2.11 1732.53 p3a4". Scaling a text model does not touch this. The letter of G3 is satisfied.

(2) THE REAL ATTACK: THE CHEAP UNTRAINED METHOD, WHICH IS EXACTLY HOW THE FOUNDER'S LAST COMPANY DIED. The competitor is not a bigger LLM, it is a few hundred lines of hand-designed optimisation with zero training data, and there have been FOUR in 24 months all aimed at precisely this failure tail: TORO (J. Appl. Cryst. 2024), FINDEXER (Struct. Dyn., Oct 2025), MCDPS-SFX (Appl. Sci. 2026), and most damagingly Nasser, Poitevin & Dalton, "Robust Indexing for Challenging Serial X-ray Diffraction Patterns" (arXiv:2511.22875, submitted 28 Nov 2025, SLAC) - which I fetched and confirmed is a CLASSICAL symmetry-aware lattice-decoding algorithm with a dedicated small-N objective mode for "patterns with few reliable peaks, background contamination, or skewed unit cells," evaluated on three XFEL protein datasets, reporting it "matches or exceeds established indexers like XGANDALF and TORO." That is the target the learned indexer must beat, it appeared while the data would still be being collected, it needs no corpus, no GPU, no labels, and it ships as a library. This is the same structural death: a general, cheap, training-free method catches up to the specialised data-heavy one.

(3) SCALE MONOTONICITY IS THIN ON MECHANISM. Ask what actually accumulates with more patterns. Orientation is uniformly random BY CONSTRUCTION (randomly oriented microcrystals in a jet) - there is no population prior over orientation to learn, ever. The unit-cell vocabulary saturates almost immediately: the entire CXIDB stream corpus is ~20 experiments covering on the order of 40 distinct cells, and within a single run the cell is constant (I measured a = 11.84 nm +/- 0.02 across 299 consecutive indexed chunks in CXIDB 152). Detector-pathology priors are facility- and geometry-specific and do not transfer across the five detector types in the archive. The only genuinely data-hungry component is a learned outlier/multi-lattice model (rejecting ice rings, jet debris, second lattices, hot pixels). That is real but unproven, and there is a live risk the curve flattens by 10^4-10^5 units, which is the "curve stops going up" failure.

**G3 — does it survive?**

PARTIALLY, and only on the modality argument.

It survives the LLM test outright and I am confident about that: the arXiv record shows the frontier-model path into crystallography is agentic tool-calling, not numerical geometry, and an agent that calls XGANDALF cannot solve patterns XGANDALF fails on - retrying with different parameters is what facilities already do. The moat against language models is real and permanent.

It does NOT cleanly survive attack (2). The best rebuttal is that the learned model amortises across a run while every classical indexer restarts a combinatorial search per pattern, so a learned prior over "which peaks belong to the target lattice" is information the classical methods structurally cannot use - and Nasser et al. themselves needed a hand-crafted "small-N objective mode," i.e. they are hand-coding by inspection the prior a model could learn from data. That is a genuine argument. But it is an argument, not evidence, and the burden is on the learned method to beat a baseline that has improved four times in two years at zero data cost.

Attack (3) I consider only weakly rebutted. The honest version of the scale story is not "indexing yield rises with patterns seen" - it is "outlier-rejection quality rises with pathology diversity seen," and the corpus has roughly 20 experiments' worth of pathology diversity, not 10^6.

**G1 — licence reality**

PASS on licence, but the proposal names the WRONG corpus and the correction matters.

LICENCE, VERIFIED: CXIDB's depositor terms state "All deposited data and metadata are made available under the CC0 waiver to promote maximum reuse" (https://www.cxidb.org/deposit.html). Every individual deposit page I scraped carries the line "Licensed under the CC0 Public Domain Dedication Waiver. Please give proper credit via citations." CC0 is a rights waiver, so redistribution of a derived (peak list -> A matrix) corpus is unambiguously permitted - Phase 3 shipping is clean. https://www.cxidb.org/robots.txt contains only Cloudflare's content-signals explanatory preamble with NO Disallow lines and NO content-signal values set, so under its own clause (c) nothing is granted or restricted; the CC0 dedication on the data files governs regardless. Crystallography Open Database: "All data in the COD and the database itself are dedicated to the public domain and licensed under the CC0 License," 534,680 entries as of 2026-08-19 (https://www.crystallography.net/cod/).

THE CORPUS CORRECTION: the proposal claims CXIDB ID 185 (~19M images) gives peak arrays already stored inside CXI files so "the working corpus is sub-GB." That is FALSE. I pulled the Data Records section of Kirkwood et al., Sci. Data 2022 via Europe PMC full text (PMC9005607, doi 10.1038/s41597-022-01266-w): "The data deposited in the Coherent X-ray imaging Data Bank (CXIDB) contains approximately 19 million images in HDF5 format... Raw data are located in the raw directory... RAW-R0083-AGIPD00-S00000.h5... The calibrated data are then stored in CORR-R0083-AGIPD00-S00000.h5." Tables 6 and 7 list only raw/calibrated AGIPD intensity, gain and mask arrays. No peak lists, no CrystFEL streams, no indexing solutions. ID 185 is multi-TB of raw detector frames and is NOT usable as a label source on a 30GB disk.

THE CORPUS THAT ACTUALLY WORKS, VERIFIED BY MEASUREMENT: I scraped all 246 CXIDB deposit pages and found 46 CrystFEL .stream files, totalling 112.6 GB as listed (e.g. CXIDB 180 large-BW-pinkIndexer.stream 31.46 GB; CXIDB 161 five streams of 1.9-10.1 GB each bz2; CXIDB 100 four streams 0.16-3.48 GB; CXIDB 62 all-amb.stream.bz2 6.09 GB; CXIDB 45 ProK-native.stream.bz2 2.84 GB), across ~20 deposits, all CC0, all served over plain HTTP with byte-range support (I range-fetched the first 4 MB of https://www.cxidb.org/data/152/KDO8PS.stream directly). Measured 445 chunks per 4 MB = 8.9 KB mean per chunk, so 112.6 GB listed (of which ~63 GB is bz2, ~5x expansion) is on the order of 10-40 million chunks, ~2/3 of them carrying an indexed crystal block with astar/bstar/cstar. That is >10^6 and plausibly >10^7 exact labelled pairs. G1 PASSES - for a different reason than the proposal gives.

**G2 — label noise and degeneracy**

HIGH, and one of the failure modes is structural rather than fixable with more data. I measured all of this on the real CXIDB 152 stream head (444 complete chunks).

(a) THE CELL LABEL IS ASSERTED, NOT DISCOVERED, AND IS CONSTANT. The stream header shows the depositor ran `indexamajig ... --indexing=xgandalf,xds,mosflm,dirax,asdf -p cubic.cell`. A target cell file was SUPPLIED. Across all 299 indexed chunks the refined cell is a = 11.84 nm +/- 0.02 with all angles 90 +/- 0.2 deg - constant to 0.2%. A constant predictor recovers the unit cell essentially perfectly within any run, and across the whole archive the cell is a ~40-way classification with heavy imbalance. Worse, the peak rows already contain a precomputed (1/d) column, so a histogram of |q| is a direct powder-ring fingerprint of the cell. Cell prediction is regex-tier.

(b) MEASURED TRIVIAL BASELINE ON THE HEADLINE METRIC. If the study frames itself as "predict indexability / raise indexing yield," I tested it: 299/444 chunks indexed = 67.3% majority-class accuracy; a SINGLE THRESHOLD on num_peaks (t >= 14) gets 81.3%. Indexed chunks have mean 27.4 peaks (median 23), non-indexed have mean 18.4 (median 12). One integer captures most of the signal. That framing is degenerate.

(c) THE FATAL ONE - SELECTION BIAS THAT BREAKS THE VPT ANALOGY. Labels exist only where a classical indexer succeeded. The thesis targets the 33-50% where it failed, and on that population there is ZERO A-matrix ground truth, by construction, forever. In VPT the label manufacturer was strictly STRONGER than the learner: the IDM saw future frames the policy never gets, so distilling it transferred real information. Here CrystFEL sees exactly the same peak list the student sees - no information asymmetry at all. A student trained to imitate CrystFEL on CrystFEL's successes converges to CrystFEL, at best. This is not a noise problem you fix with more patterns; it is the wrong shape of manufacturer.

(d) EVALUATION AMBIGUITY. The A matrix is defined only modulo the lattice point group (48 equivalent orientations for m-3m) and modulo indexing ambiguity - CXIDB 203's own README instructs users to run `ambigator -y m-3 -w m-3m`, and CXIDB 152's stream was itself re-indexed by ambigator. Naive matrix-MSE loss or naive angular error will be systematically wrong unless quotiented, and getting that wrong silently inflates or destroys the reported numbers.

WHAT PARTIALLY REDEEMS IT: the non-indexed chunks ARE deposited with their full peak lists (145 of 444 here), so the failure population is available as input even without labels; and merging statistics (CC1/2, CC*, Rsplit against the deposited MTZ/PDB) are a genuine free environment-supplied validator - indexing garbage degrades them - so a real-data claim can be checked without per-pattern labels. The forward-simulation arm from COD does produce labels on the hard tail, but the moment you lean on it you are training on synthetics, which is exactly LaueNN and AIdex territory, and the sim-to-real gap is precisely the pathologies nobody can simulate well.

**G6 — CPU feasibility, concretely**

Comfortably feasible; this is the strongest gate. All numbers derived from measurement, not estimate.

UNIT SIZE. Measured on the real stream: mean 8.9 KB of TEXT per chunk (min 1.05 KB, max 64 KB), 24.4 peaks mean (min 10, max 246), 126.7 predicted reflections per indexed chunk. Parsed to a compact binary record: peaks as (fs, ss, 1/d, I) x float32 = 16 B/peak. At the measured mean of 24 peaks that is 384 B; capping at 200 peaks worst-case gives 3.2 KB. Label = 9 float32 A matrix (astar/bstar/cstar) = 36 B, plus lattice_type, centering, indexed_by flag and a run/geometry id = ~32 B. Call it ~450 B mean, ~1 KB with padding overhead.

CORPUS AT 10^6 UNITS. ~0.45 GB packed, ~1-3 GB if stored padded to a fixed 200-peak tensor. Against 30 GB writable disk that leaves ample room for all four rungs, a simulated arm, and shards. Ingest cost: 10^6 chunks x 8.9 KB = ~9 GB of stream text, i.e. two or three of the mid-size deposits, or piped as `curl | bunzip2 | parser` so nothing large ever lands on disk. Hours of network, not days. (Note: harvesting the FULL 112.6 GB would be a multi-day network job and one single file, CXIDB 180's 31.46 GB uncompressed stream, exceeds the disk - so streaming-and-reducing is mandatory, not optional.)

MODEL. Point-set / set-transformer over the peak set, d_model = 128, 4 layers, 4 heads: ~1.5M parameters. Params + Adam moments = ~25 MB. Activations at batch 256 with N padded to 64 peaks: ~100 MB. Total well under 1 GB against 15 GB RAM - the box is over-provisioned by an order of magnitude.

CPU-HOURS FOR A 4-RUNG CURVE ON 4 CORES. Per-sample forward FLOPs at N = 64: 4 layers x (4·N·d^2 projections + 2·N^2·d attention) = 4 x (4·64·16384 + 2·4096·128) ~= 4 x (4.2M + 1.05M) ~= 21 MFLOP; forward+backward ~= 63 MFLOP. Four cores with AVX2 fp32 through PyTorch/MKL realistically deliver 8-20 GFLOP/s sustained; taking a conservative 10 GFLOP/s gives ~160 samples/s, and small-N attention is latency- not FLOP-bound so 150-400/s is the realistic band.
Rung schedule: 1k x 200 epochs = 2x10^5 steps; 10k x 100 = 1x10^6; 100k x 40 = 4x10^6; 1M x 10 = 1x10^7. Total ~= 1.5x10^7 sample-steps. At 200 samples/s that is ~21 hours wall-clock for one full curve. With three seeds at the small rungs, the COD-simulated arm, and evaluation, budget 2-3 days. That is inside "within days."

No GPU is needed anywhere: the label manufacturer is already-computed CrystFEL output (pure parsing, no re-indexing), the simulation arm is analytic reciprocal-space geometry, and the model is small enough that CPU attention over 24-200 points is cheap. G6 is not the constraint here.

**Strongest case against**

The label manufacturer is definitionally blind to the population the thesis is about, and it is not stronger than the learner - which is the one property that made VPT work.

Every free label comes from a classical indexer succeeding. The product claim is "index the 33-50% that classical indexers discard." There is therefore no ground truth, and no way to manufacture ground truth from the real archive, on the exact patterns the buyer cares about. Unlike VPT - where the IDM saw future frames and so genuinely knew something the student's input could not contain - CrystFEL here sees the identical peak list the student sees. Distilling it has an information ceiling equal to CrystFEL itself. The escape hatch is forward simulation from COD, but that converts the study into synthetic-trained lattice recovery, which is what LaueNN and AIdex already are, and moves the entire risk into a sim-to-real gap over precisely the pathologies (multi-lattice, ice, jet debris, partiality, wrong-cell contaminants) that nobody knows how to simulate faithfully - because if they did, the patterns would already index.

Compounding it: I measured that the obvious headline metric is near-degenerate (81.3% indexability accuracy from a single num_peaks threshold vs 67.3% majority), the cell half of the label is supplied by the depositor via `-p cubic.cell` and is constant to 0.2% within a run, the orientation half is uniformly random by construction so there is no population prior to accumulate, and the cell vocabulary across the whole archive is ~40 - so it is genuinely unclear what keeps improving from 10^5 to 10^6 units. Meanwhile four training-free classical indexers have attacked this same failure tail since 2024, the newest (arXiv:2511.22875, Nov 2025, SLAC) reporting it matches or exceeds XGANDALF and TORO with zero data. That is the founder's previous death replayed with a different general method.
